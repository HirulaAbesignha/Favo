Abstract 
University campuses encompass a diverse ecosystem of services that students and staff interact with on 
a daily basis including academic support, parking management, food ordering, lost property recovery, 
study space allocation, and peer -to-peer commerce. T he fragmented nature of these services, each 
operating through separate manual processes or isolated digital tools, creates significant friction and 
inefficiency in the campus experience. 
 
This report presents the design and development of the Smart University Support Platform a unified, 
AI/ML-enhanced mobile application developed for Sri Lanka Institute of Information Technology 
(SLIIT). The platform consolidates six critical campus service s into a single, cohesive application: 
Smart Study Support, Parking Management, Food Corner, Lost and Found, Study Areas, and 
Marketplace. 
 
The AI/ML core of the system comprises three tightly integrated intelligent components. First, an AI -
powered Semantic Search engine in the Lost and Found module employs vector embedding similarity 
techniques to match user descriptions or existing posts aga inst the full catalogue of reported items, 
enabling accurate retrieval beyond simple keyword matching. Second, an AI Product Suggestion engine 
in the Marketplace Buyer module uses content-based filtering to match a natural language description 
of a user's need against active listings, surfacing relevant items without requiring exact search terms. 
Third, a Study Support Grouping mechanism applies clustering algorithms to identify and group 
semantically similar academic requests, enabling tutors to address mu ltiple students with shared 
confusion in a single session rather than responding to each individually. 
 
Beyond its AI capabilities, the platform provides a comprehensive Smart Study Support module for 
submitting and tracking tutoring requests; a Campus Parking Management system with a visual lot 
layout and vehicle registration; a Food Corner for browsing canteen menus and placing orders; a Study 
Areas tracker with real-time GPS-based occupancy detection; and a Marketplace supporting both buyer 
and seller workflows with AI-assisted product discovery. 
 
The system was evaluated through functional testing across all six modules, AI/ML model performance 
assessment, and user feedback collection. Results demonstrate strong functional coverage, measurable 
AI prediction accuracy, and high usability scores from simulated stakeholder evaluations. 
 
Keywords: Campus Management, Semantic Search, Vector Embeddings, Recommendation System,

Chapter 1: Introduction 
1.1 Problem and Motivation 
University campus environments are characterized by a wide array of parallel services that students 
must navigate independently. At the Sri Lanka Institute of Information Technology (SLIIT), students 
routinely require access to academic tutoring support, c ampus parking, canteen meal ordering, lost 
property resolution, study space information, and peer -to-peer buying and selling. These services are 
presently managed through disconnected channels physical noticeboards, WhatsApp groups, verbal 
communication, and standalone applications creating a fragmented and inefficient experience for the 
university community. 
Several specific pain points motivate this project. First, academic support requests are submitted 
individually to lecturers, who must then respond to dozens of students who may share identical 
conceptual difficulties. Without a mechanism to identify and g roup similar requests, tutoring sessions 
are duplicated and inefficient. Second, campus parking is managed manually; students lack real -time 
visibility into slot availability, resulting in time wasted circling the lot, and the absence of digital 
registration creates accountability gaps. Third, food ordering at canteens requires physical queuing 
during peak hours, increasing congestion and wait times. Fourth, lost items reported through social 
media channels are searched manually, with no intelligent matching between a finder's description and 
a seeker's description. Fifth, study area occupancy particularly in the library and designated zones is not 
communicated digitally, requiring students to physically scout for available space. Sixth, student -to-
student commerce for textbooks, electronics, and equipment has no trusted, campus-specific platform. 
The motivation for this project is rooted in the opportunity to address these six main pain points through 
a single intelligent mobile application that applies AI/ML to the most friction -intensive aspects of 
campus life. 
 
1.2 Literature Review 
The application of Natural Language Processing and semantic similarity techniques to item retrieval 
has been extensively studied in the context of information retrieval systems. Mikolov et al. [1] 
introduced word embedding models that capture semantic rela tionships between terms, enabling 
similarity searches that extend beyond exact keyword matching. Subsequent work by Reimers and 
Gurevych [2] with Sentence-BERT demonstrated that transformer-based sentence embeddings provide 
superior semantic similarity per formance for paraphrase matching and duplicate detection tasks a 
capability directly relevant to matching lost item descriptions submitted by different users. 
Recommendation systems for e -commerce applications have been studied by Ricci et al. [3], who 
established that content-based filtering where item attributes are matched to user-expressed preferences 
achieves strong precision for small, domain-specific catalogues such as a university marketplace. This 
approach avoids the cold-start problem inherent in collaborative filtering when user interaction history 
is sparse. 
The clustering of academic support requests relates directly to the work of Agrawal and Pant [4], who 
applied K-Means and hierarchical clustering to student query datasets and demonstrated that up to 68% 
of support requests could be addressed by a single r esponse targeting a cluster centroid topic. This

system identifies when multiple students have submitted thematically equivalent requests. 
Real-time occupancy detection using GPS has been studied in the context of smart campus systems by

configurable radius parameters can achieve reliable indoor-outdoor occupancy classification for room-

1.3 Aim and Objectives

The aim of this project is to design, develop, and evaluate the Smart University Support Platform an 
AI/ML-powered mobile campus services application for SLIIT that unifies academic support, parking 
management, food ordering, lost and found, study area tra cking, and student marketplace operations

lost item matching, and product recommendations.

1.4 Solution Overview 
The Smart University Support Platform is a full-stack mobile application designed for the SLIIT campus 
community. The system is organized into six primary service modules, each addressing a distinct 
campus operational domain, and an AI/ML subsystem that en hances three of these modules with 
intelligent automation. 
The Smart Study Support module provides a student-facing dashboard for submitting academic support 
requests, specifying topic, availability slots, and description. The system groups similar requests using 
NLP clustering and presents them to tutors for batc h resolution. Students can track the status of their 
requests from submission through scheduling to completion. 
The Parking Management module presents a live visual map of all parking lot sections Car Park Side 
A, Car Park Side B, and Motorcycle Parking with colour-coded slot availability (green: available, red: 
occupied, blue: your slot). Students register their vehicles and tap a slot to park; slots auto-release after 
24 hours. 
The Food Corner module allows students to select a canteen and browse its menu with item images and 
prices. Orders are managed through a cart and orders history interface. 
The Lost and Found module enables students to post found or lost items with descriptions, images, and 
location tags. An AI Search feature accepts a natural language description or an existing post as 
reference and returns semantically similar listings, dramatically improving the probability of reuniting 
owners with their lost property. 
The Study Areas module uses real -time GPS location to determine which study zones are currently 
occupied by registered students. A campus -wide list shows each area with its current student count, 
capacity, and occupancy status (Free, Moderate, Crowded). St udents' tracking status is active as long 
as the app is running. 
The Marketplace module supports both buyer and seller workflows. Sellers create listings with images, 
descriptions, and prices and manage incoming purchase requests. Buyers browse listings sorted by 
recency or price, and can use the AI Product Suggestions feature to describe their need in natural 
language and receive intelligently matched listing recommendations.

Chapter 3: Design and Development 
3.1 System Architecture 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Figure 3.1: System Architecture Diagram 
The Smart University Support Platform is designed as a three -tier layered architecture comprising a 
Presentation Layer (React Native mobile application), an Application Layer (REST API backend 
services), and a Data Layer (MongoDB Atlas database and Python AI microservices). This architectural 
pattern ensures separation of concerns, enabling independent development, testing, and deployment of 
each tier. 
The Presentation Layer is a React Native mobile application delivering a unified interface across 
Android and iOS platforms. It provides module -specific screens for each of the six service domains, 
with a shared authentication layer and a home screen modul e selector. The application communicates 
with the backend exclusively through REST API calls. 
The Application Layer is organized into six primary service modules — Study Support Service, Parking 
Service, Food Service, Lost and Found Service, Study Area Service, and Marketplace Service — each 
encapsulating the business logic for its respective domai n. A dedicated AI/ML Microservice handles 
all machine learning workflows: semantic embedding computation, similarity search, recommendation 
generation, and request clustering. An Authentication Service manages user sessions and role -based 
access using JWT (JSON Web Tokens). 
The Data Layer comprises MongoDB Atlas as the primary operational database for storing all 
application data across service domains, a cloud storage bucket for item images (Lost and Found and

Marketplace), and a Python AI microservice environment maintaining pre -computed embedding 
indices for fast similarity retrieval. 
 
3.1.1 Component Architecture Description 
 
 
 
 
 
  
 
Figure 3.2: Component Architecture Diagram 
The central integration point of the system is the shared Authentication context, which gates access to 
all six modules. The Study Support module feeds student request data into the AI Grouping Engine, 
which returns cluster labels that the tutor dashboard uses to present grouped views. The Lost and Found 
module feeds item descriptions into the Semantic Search Engine at query time, returning ranked similar 
results. The Marketplace Buyer module submits natural language need descriptions to the Product 
Suggestion Engine, which returns relevant listing IDs ranked by content similarity. 
The AI/ML microservice follows a pipeline architecture: Raw Text Input → Tokenisation → Sentence 
Embedding (Sentence -BERT / TF -IDF) → Similarity Computation (cosine similarity) → Ranked 
Result Return. For the Study Support Grouping pipeline, the flow is: Batch of Requests → Embedding 
→ K-Means Clustering → Cluster Label Assignment → Tutor Dashboard Notification.

3.2.1 AI Lost and Found Search Workflow

The AI Lost and Found search workflow is initiated when a student either types a natural language 
description of their item or selects an existing post as a reference. The system submits this input to the 
Semantic Search Engine, which computes a sentence e mbedding vector using the pre -trained model. 
This query vector is compared against pre-computed embeddings for all active listings in the catalogue 
using cosine similarity. Items are ranked by similarity score and returned to the user as an ordered result 
list. The system filters out the query post itself if a reference post was used as input. 
 
3.2.2 Study Support Request Grouping Workflow 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Figure 3.4: Study Support Grouping Workflow 
The Study Support Grouping workflow is triggered when a new request is submitted or on a scheduled 
batch basis. The AI Grouping Engine embeds all pending requests for a given topic area and applies K-
Means clustering to identify groups of semantically simi lar requests. Requests assigned to the same 
cluster are tagged with a 'GROUPED' status, and the cluster topic is surfaced to the assigned tutor along 
with the count of affected students. The tutor can then schedule a single session to address the shared 
conceptual difficulty, and all grouped students are notified simultaneously.

The AI Product Suggestion workflow begins when a buyer enters a natural language description of their 
need into the suggestion input field. The system embeds this description and computes its cosine

title and description in its similarity computation, ensuring that conceptually relevant items are surfaced 
even when the exact terminology differs from the buyer's query.

3.3.1 Core Collections and Relationships 
The Users collection stores all registered user profiles with fields for user ID, name, email address, 
hashed password reference, assigned role (Student, BatchRep, Admin, Canteen Owner), and registration 
timestamp. 
The SupportRequests collection captures each academic support request with fields for topic code, 
subtopic description, submitting user ID, availability slots (array of date -time pairs), current status 
(Pending, Grouped, Scheduled, Completed), cluster labe l, and session reference. Cluster labels are 
written by the AI Grouping Engine after processing. 
The ParkingSlots collection maintains the state of every parking slot in the campus lots, with fields for 
slot ID, section code (A, B, BK), status (Available, Occupied), occupying user ID, and occupation 
timestamp. An auto-release scheduled background job monitors occupation timestamps and resets slots 
after 24 hours. 
The Canteens and MenuItems collections support the Food Corner module. Each Menu Item  record 
stores canteen reference, item name, description, price, availability flag, and image URL. Orders are 
stored in a separate Orders collection referencing user, can teen, item list, quantities, total price, and 
status. 
The LostFoundItems collection stores each posting with fields for title, description, category, location 
tags, posting user, item status (Open, Resolved, Lost, Found), image, and a pre -computed embedding 
vector for AI search. The embedding is computed and stored at post creation time to enable fast 
similarity retrieval without recomputing at query time. 
The StudyAreas collection maintains definitions of all campus study zones with fields for area name, 
description, GPS coordinates (latitude, longitude), radius in metres, capacity, and current occupancy 
count. A Checkins sub -collection tracks active check -ins per area. A backend service monitors GPS 
proximity and writes/deletes check-in records automatically. 
The MarketplaceListings collection stores each listing with fields for seller user ID, title, description, 
price, category, status (Active, Sold, Removed), image, and a pre -computed embedding vector. 
BuyerRequests are stored as a sub -collection referencing the buyer, message, contact number, and 
request status. 
 
3.4 AI/ML Development 
3.4.1 AI Semantic Search Lost and Found (UC-701) 
The Semantic Search Engine for the Lost and Found module (UC-701) is the first AI component of the 
Smart University Support Platform. Its purpose is to retrieve listings that are semantically similar to a 
user's query whether expressed as a free -text descr iption or by selecting an existing post enabling 
accurate item matching even when the finder's and seeker's descriptions use different vocabulary. 
Algorithm Selection: The engine employs Sentence-BERT (SBERT), a fine-tuned BERT variant that 
produces fixed-length dense vector embeddings for input sentences while preserving semantic meaning. 
SBERT was selected because it provides significantly superior semantic similarity performance 
compared to TF-IDF bag-of-words approaches for short, descriptive texts typical of lost item reports 
(e.g., 'black Samsung phone with cracked screen' matching 'Samsung A56 mobile phone with black 
Smart University Support Platform | IT2021 Final Report | WE-DS-01-G03 
Page 13 of 37 
back cover'). The all -MiniLM-L6-v2 model variant was used, providing a strong balance between 
embedding quality and computational efficiency. 
Embedding Pipeline: At item posting time, the item title and description are concatenated and passed 
through the SBERT model to produce a 384 -dimensional embedding vector. This vector is stored 
alongside the item record in MongoDB. At query time, the user's input text is emb edded in real time 
using the same model, and cosine similarity is computed between the query vector and all stored item 
embeddings. Items are ranked by descending similarity score and returned to the UI. 
Evaluation: The engine was evaluated on a test set of 50 manually matched query -item pairs derived 
from anonymized descriptions. Precision@5 (the proportion of the top-5 results containing the relevant 
item) was measured at 0.82, indicating that in 82% of test cases t he correct item appeared in the top 5 
results. This performance represents a substantial improvement over a keyword baseline, which 
achieved Precision@5 of 0.48 on the same test set. 
 
3.4.2 AI Product Suggestion Marketplace (UC-702) 
The AI Product Suggestion Engine for the Marketplace Buyer module (UC -702) enables students to 
describe their need in natural language and receive intelligently matched listing recommendations, 
bypassing the limitations of keyword search for a domain where sellers may describe items differently 
from how buyers express their needs. 
Algorithm Selection: The engine applies the same SBERT embedding approach used in the Lost and 
Found module, computing cosine similarity between the buyer's natural language query and the pre -
computed embeddings of all active marketplace listings. Content -based filtering was s elected over 
collaborative filtering because the marketplace catalogue is relatively small and user interaction history 
is sparse at launch, making collaborative approaches susceptible to the cold-start problem. 
Feature Representation: Each listing's embedding is computed from the concatenation of its title, 
description, and category label at listing creation time. This composite text representation ensures that 
both the item type and its specific attributes contribute to the embedding, improving retrieval for queries 
that specify both the product category and desired characteristics (e.g., 'lightweight laptop for coding 
with good battery' matching 'Dell Inspiron 15 with Intel i5, 8GB RAM, 512GB SSD'). 
Evaluation: The suggestion engine was tested on 40 natural language queries against a catalogue of 25 
active listings. Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR) the reciprocal of the rank of the first relevant result was 
measured at 0.71, indicating strong ranking quality. User eval uators rated the relevance of top -3 
suggestions at an average of 4.1/5 in structured feedback sessions. 
 
3.4.3 Study Support Request Grouping (UC-703) 
The Study Support Grouping mechanism (UC -703) addresses the academic inefficiency of tutors 
responding to identical conceptual questions from multiple students independently. The system 
identifies clusters of semantically equivalent requests and flags them for batch resolution. 
Algorithm Selection: The grouping pipeline employs a two-stage approach. First, all pending requests 
within a topic area are embedded using TF -IDF vectorization followed by Truncated SVD for 
dimensionality reduction a computationally lighter approach than SBERT appropriate for  short, 
Smart University Support Platform | IT2021 Final Report | WE-DS-01-G03 
Page 14 of 37 
structured academic queries that tend to use domain -specific terminology consistently. Second, 
Agglomerative Hierarchical Clustering with cosine distance is applied to the reduced embedding space. 
The dendrogram is cut at a configurable distance threshold (default: 0.4) to form clusters. 
Grouping Logic: Requests assigned to the same cluster are marked with status 'GROUPED' and linked 
to the cluster centroid. The tutor dashboard surfaces each cluster as a single card showing the shared 
topic, representative description, and the count of students affected. Tutors may accept the grouping 
and schedule a single session, or override the grouping and schedule individual responses. 
Evaluation: The grouping mechanism was evaluated on a dataset of 120 synthetic support requests 
across 6 topic areas. Clustering quality was measured using the Silhouette Score, which achieved 0.61, 
indicating well -separated clusters. Manual review of 30 randomly samp led cluster assignments by 
subject-matter evaluators confirmed correct grouping in 87% of cases. 
 
3.4.4 GPS-Based Study Area Occupancy Tracking 
The Study Area Occupancy tracking system determines in real time which registered students are 
present within each defined campus study zone, enabling the live occupancy counts displayed on the 
Study Areas screen. 
Each study area is defined by a GPS coordinate center and a radius in metres (configurable per area; 
current values range from 5 m to 20 m). The mobile application uses the device's GPS/location service 
to emit periodic location updates while the app is in  the foreground or background (with appropriate 
permissions). A client -side geofence check evaluates the Haversine distance between the device's 
current coordinates and each area's centre. when a student enters the defined study area boundary, the 
system detects it using GPS and marks it as an entry (check-in). When the student leaves the area, the 
system detects the exit and removes the check-in record. The number of students inside the study area 
is calculated based on how many active check-in records are currently available. This count is then used 
to show the status as Free, Moderate, or Crowded.

Chapter 5: Conclusion 
5.1 Summary of Achievements 
This project successfully delivered the Smart University Support Platform an AI/ML -powered mobile 
campus services application meeting all eight project objectives defined in Section 1.3.2 of this report. 
The system was designed, developed, and evaluated within the academic context of the IT2021 module 
at SLIIT. 
The core technical contributions of this project are threefold. First, the development of a complete, 
integrated mobile application that unifies six critical campus service domains within a single platform, 
eliminating the fragmentation and friction associated with the current multi-channel approach. Second, 
the implementation of three AI/ML components semantic search, product suggestion, and request 
grouping that deliver measurable, quantified improvements over baseline non -AI approaches. Third, 
the demons tration that transformer -based sentence embeddings provide practical, production -viable 
semantic understanding for the short, informal text descriptions typical of campus service applications, 
without requiring domain-specific training data. 
 
5.2 Achievement of Aim and Objectives 
The project aim to design, develop, and evaluate an AI/ML -powered campus services application that 
unifies six service domains while embedding intelligent automation for study support, lost item 
retrieval, and marketplace discovery has been fully achieved.  The system delivers all specified 
functional capabilities, passes 96.6% of structured functional tests, and achieves strong AI component 
performance with Precision@5 of 0.82 for semantic search, MRR of 0.71 for product suggestions, and 
a 63% reduction in required tutor responses through grouping. 
Each of the eight objectives has been met: the multi -module mobile application is fully implemented 
across all six service domains; the Semantic Search engine is trained, validated, and deployed with 
documented performance metrics; the AI Product Suggestio n engine is implemented and evaluated 
against natural language queries; the Study Support Grouping mechanism is deployed and evaluated on 
synthetic and manual test data; GPS-based occupancy tracking operates in real time for all defined study 
areas; the Parking Management system supports vehicle registration and visual slot selection; the Food 
Corner supports multi -canteen browsing and ordering; and the complete system has been evaluated 
through functional testing, AI performance assessment, and user feedback collection. 
 
5.3 Future Work 
Several directions for future development have been identified through the testing and evaluation phase. 
The implementation of push notifications for parking reminders, food order status updates, and study 
session scheduling confirmations would substantially improve the real-time utility of the platform for 
daily campus use. 
An in-app messaging system between Lost and Found posters and responders, and between Marketplace 
buyers and sellers, would reduce the current reliance on external communication channels (WhatsApp) 
and improve transaction trust and safety within the campus context.
